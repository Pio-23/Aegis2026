AEGIS EXPLORATION LESSONS

1. EXPLORE WITH PURPOSE.
Maintain a route objective. In a sufficiently clear hallway,
prefer continued forward progress over frequent direction changes.

2. USE LONGER FORWARD SEGMENTS WHEN VERIFIED SAFE.
A useful target is 6-8 seconds at moderate speed in open corridors.
This is not a minimum. Shorten or stop when clearance is uncertain.
Each segment still requires independent obstacle supervision.

3. DO NOT OSCILLATE.
Avoid turning LEFT then RIGHT repeatedly without new evidence or
meaningful progress. Consider a supported alternative route.

4. REUSE 360-DEGREE LIDAR.
A turn in place does not erase the previous scan's geometry.
Account for estimated heading changes, but remember IMU yaw can
be inaccurate. Rescan when fresh information is genuinely useful,
not automatically after every turn.

5. UNDERSTAND SIDE OPENINGS.
Front-left and front-right ultrasonic sensors are front-facing.
They do not prove whether the left or right SIDE is clear.
Use full 360-degree LiDAR geometry and rover footprint checks.

6. TRACK ACTUAL PROGRESS CAREFULLY.
Commanded movement duration is not measured travel distance.
Interrupted movements and inaccurate heading can cause map drift.

7. RECOGNIZE UNCERTAINTY.
Do not assume missing LiDAR returns indicate open space.
Do not recommend turning unless the rover's swept footprint
has enough clearance. Explain what information is missing.

8. AVOID UNNECESSARY INACTION.
If the front is blocked but a measured, sufficiently wide side
passage exists, consider turning toward it instead of endlessly
requesting scans or NO_OP.

9. LEARN FROM REVIEWED EXAMPLES.
A human-reviewed success or failure is useful evidence.
Do not treat every Luna decision or every Gemma prediction as
automatically correct.
